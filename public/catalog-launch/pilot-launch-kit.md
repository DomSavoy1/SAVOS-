# Savoy Catalog Rescue pilot launch kit

Proposed service under Savoy Ventures. This kit is ready for owner review. Pricing, capacity, business identity, and delivery commitments remain unvalidated. No outreach has been sent and no orders have been accepted.

## The customer and problem

Start with small retailers or the agencies supporting them whose product launch is delayed by inconsistent supplier data. The buying trigger is a specific blocked upload or handoff. A prospect with no current catalog problem is a poor fit, even if it operates an online store.

## Offer copy

### Get your product data ready for the next handoff

Savoy Catalog Rescue organizes a small batch of product records into your agreed catalog format. You receive the cleaned file, a record of changes, and a clear list of missing information that needs your decision.

The proposed $250 pilot covers one source file and up to 25 product or variant records, with one revision. Each variant counts as a record. We confirm the source quality, required fields, and delivery date before accepting the work.

Send a representative sample and the required destination format to request a scope review. Remove customer, payment, and other unrelated personal information before sharing a sample.

## Scope to confirm before accepting payment

- One client-supplied source file, one destination format, and up to 25 total product or variant records.
- Up to eight agreed fields, such as SKU, title, category, color, size, price, currency, and image reference.
- Whitespace cleanup, agreed naming conventions, exact duplicate detection, required-field checks, and consistency checks against supplied facts.
- Deliver a cleaned file, change log, exception list, and handoff notes.
- One consolidated revision within five business days of delivery, limited to the original scope. Errors introduced by our work should be corrected without treating them as an expanded scope.
- Proposed target: delivery within three business days after complete inputs and scope acceptance. Confirm capacity before promising this.

Exclude live store imports, inventory or accounting reconciliation, translation, image production, original product research, and discovering missing facts from the web. Quote those separately only if there is proven ability and capacity to deliver them. Do not promise increased sales or platform acceptance without actual verification.

## Intake questions

1. What launch or workflow is blocked, and what is its deadline?
2. Which system will receive this data, and can you provide its current template or your developer's specification?
3. How many rows and variants are involved, and which fields must be changed?
4. Which source is authoritative when values disagree?
5. What formatting conventions should be preserved, especially SKU case and leading zeros?
6. Who will answer missing-information questions and approve the finished file?
7. Can you provide a small representative sample that includes a difficult record?
8. Who will perform the final import, and how will they test it before changing production data?

## Fictional work sample

This example demonstrates the proposed method. It is not a customer result or a Shopify import file. Assume the fictional client has explicitly approved title case, uppercase color labels, trimmed outer whitespace, and USD as the currency for supplied prices. No convention below should be applied automatically to a real client's data.

| Row | Field | Input | Proposed output | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | SKU | ` 00127 ` | `00127` | Remove outer spaces and preserve leading zeros |
| 1 | Title | `cotton tote` | `Cotton Tote` | Apply approved title style |
| 1 | Color | ` navy ` | `NAVY` | Apply approved color vocabulary |
| 1 | Price | `18.00` | `18.00` | Preserve supplied amount |
| 2 | SKU | `00128` | `00128` | Preserve identifier |
| 2 | Price | blank | blank with exception | Request the amount; never infer it |
| 3 | SKU | `00127` | held for review | Repeated SKU alone does not prove an exact duplicate |

Example exception report: Row 2 requires a price from the client. Row 3 repeats row 1's SKU but has a different color; the client must confirm whether the SKU or variant relationship is wrong. Neither row is marked ready for import.

## Delivery quality checks

Keep the original input unchanged. Record input and output row counts and explain any difference. Preserve identifiers as text. Check that each changed value follows an agreed rule or supplied source. Count missing required values, conflicting repeated identifiers, and invalid field formats before and after cleanup. Never report an unresolved issue as fixed. Review every row of a 25-record pilot. Ensure unresolved rows are clearly separated or flagged according to the agreed destination format. Have the receiving person verify the sample against their import requirements before a production change.

## Paid validation and decision rules

Build a list of 20 prospects with an observable trigger and record why each fits. Obtain owner authorization before sending messages. Aim to speak with five qualified prospects and sell three pilots; these are experiment targets, not predicted conversion rates. Record objections in the prospect's own words. Do not buy ads or increase software spending to compensate for an offer with no customer interest.

Track each prospect's source, observed need, contact date, reply, qualification, quoted scope, paid amount, delivery hours, revision hours, cash costs, refund, and outcome. Keep total selling and administration time alongside delivery time.

Proceed toward a standard $650 package only if paid work demonstrates a repeatable outcome and delivery capacity. Set its record count from measured difficulty and time rather than assuming four times the pilot volume takes four times the effort. If customers will pay only commodity hourly rates, test a different segment or offer before scaling.

## Weekly economics

Two $650 projects produce $1,300 revenue. A $1,000 cash operating profit target requires all business expenses for that week to remain at or below $300, including payment fees, software, acquisition, contractors, refunds, and rework costs. This excludes personal taxes and a wage for the owner's time.

If the owner spends 20 hours weekly and values that time at $25 per hour, the same scenario produces $500 after that labor allowance. To generate $1,000 after a $500 owner labor allowance and $300 other expenses requires $1,800 revenue, assuming those expenses remain fixed. Three $650 projects yield $1,950, but the additional capacity must be demonstrated.

Validate the income goal using actual collected revenue and recorded expenses over at least four consecutive weeks. Track booked work separately from cash collected. An average of $1,000 does not prove every week reaches $1,000; report both the average and the lowest week.
